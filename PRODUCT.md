# Product

## Register

product

## Users

A single household's people (e.g. Felipe, Ana) tracking shared and individual finances. No login, no accounts — the household is fixed and every record belongs to one person. Each person can view their own expenses solo or see the household's combined view. Usage is check-in style: log a transaction, review the monthly view, check installments and dashboard cards, mostly on desktop and mobile.

## Product Purpose

HomeLedger gives a household one place to track people, incomes, expenses, categories, recurring transactions, credit card purchases and installments, and monthly/annual financial views. Success looks like: at a glance, know what's been spent, what's still owed (installments), and how the month/year is trending — per person and combined.

## Brand Personality

Calm, trustworthy, precise. This is a finance tool, not a game — no gamification, no forced cheerfulness. Numbers read cleanly, states are unambiguous, nothing distracts from the task of understanding the household's money.

## Anti-references

No hero-metric SaaS dashboard clichés, no gradient text, no confetti/celebration UI for spending. Avoid making it feel like a marketing product — this is a private household tool, not something being sold.

## Design Principles

- Numbers first: amounts, dates, and totals must be the most legible thing on any screen.
- Unambiguous state: income vs. expense, paid vs. unpaid, must never rely on color alone.
- Solo and shared views are equally first-class — switching between "my expenses" and "household" should feel like the same app, not two modes bolted together.
- Calm over flashy: motion and color are used to clarify, not to decorate.
- Respect existing tokens: the app already has a considered emerald/dark palette — extend it, don't replace it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, large text ≥3:1. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Income/expense and paid/unpaid states must be distinguishable without color (icon, sign, or label), not hue alone.
